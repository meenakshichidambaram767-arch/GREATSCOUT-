# GreatScout — Opportunity Scoring Methodology

**Tagline:** Scout Smart, Build Better.  
**Model Version:** `2.1-transparent-7pillar`

---

## 1. Core Philosophy

A central design principle of GreatScout is **scientific transparency**: an opportunity score must never be an arbitrary black-box number generated by an ungrounded LLM. 

To provide genuine market intelligence for founders, investors, and product managers:
1. Every score is composed of **7 distinct dimensions**.
2. **Empirical evidence metrics** (85% aggregate weight) are strictly separated from **heuristic estimates** (15% aggregate weight).
3. The platform displays metric types explicitly (`Measured` vs `Heuristic Estimate`) on all opportunity dossiers.
4. All scores are bounded between `0.0` and `100.0`.

---

## 2. Seven Scoring Pillars

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        COMPOSITE OPPORTUNITY GAP SCORE (0 - 100)                       │
│                                                                                        │
│   Empirical Evidence Metrics (85% Weight)          Heuristic & Recency (15% Weight)    │
│   ├── Demand Volume (25%)                          ├── Technical Feasibility (5%)      │
│   ├── Pain Severity (25%)                          └── Evidence Recency Decay (5%)     │
│   ├── Evidence Quality & Density (15%)                                                 │
│   ├── Willingness to Pay / Commercial Intent (15%)                                     │
│   └── Competitive Vacuum (10%)                                                         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. Mathematical Formulations

### Pillar 1: Demand Volume Score ($S_{\text{demand}}$) — Weight: 25% (Measured)
Reflects the frequency and recurrence of the problem across discussions:
$$S_{\text{demand}} = \min\left(100.0, \, \min(92.0, 45.0 + 16.0 \cdot N) + B_{\text{diversity}}\right)$$
Where:
- $N$ is the number of validated signals in the cluster.
- $B_{\text{diversity}} = 8.0$ if the signals originate from more than one platform (e.g., Reddit + Product Hunt), otherwise $0.0$.

### Pillar 2: Pain Severity Score ($S_{\text{pain}}$) — Weight: 25% (Measured)
Reflects user emotional distress and operational impact:
$$S_{\text{pain}} = \min\left(100.0, \, \max\left(20.0, \, \left(\frac{\bar{Sev}}{5.0} \cdot 80.0\right) + \min(20.0, -\bar{Sent} \cdot 20.0)\right)\right)$$
Where:
- $\bar{Sev} \in [1, 5]$ is the average severity score across cluster signals.
- $\bar{Sent} \in [-1.0, 1.0]$ is the average sentiment polarity (more negative yields a higher pain bonus).

### Pillar 3: Evidence Strength & Density ($S_{\text{evidence}}$) — Weight: 15% (Measured)
Evaluates quotation detail and evidentiary corroboration:
$$S_{\text{evidence}} = \min\left(100.0, \, \max\left(25.0, \, \min\left(50.0, \frac{\bar{L}_{\text{quote}}}{120} \cdot 50.0\right) + \min(50.0, N \cdot 15.0)\right)\right)$$
Where:
- $\bar{L}_{\text{quote}}$ is the average character length of cited verbatim quotes.
- $N$ is the sample size of signals.

### Pillar 4: Willingness-to-Pay / Commercial Intent ($S_{\text{wtp}}$) — Weight: 15% (Measured)
Quantifies whether users express a budget, revenue loss, or urgent commercial intent:
$$S_{\text{wtp}} = \min\left(100.0, \, \max\left(25.0, \, 40.0 + (40.0 \cdot \max(C_i)) + (20.0 \cdot \bar{C})\right)\right)$$
Where:
- $C_i \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the commercial intent score of individual signals.
- $\max(C_i)$ captures peak commercial urgency, while $\bar{C}$ ensures sustained intent across the cluster.

### Pillar 5: Competitive Vacuum Score ($S_{\text{vacuum}}$) — Weight: 10% (Measured)
Evaluates whether users cite dissatisfaction with existing alternatives:
$$S_{\text{vacuum}} = \begin{cases} 
\min(100.0, \, 75.0 + 8.0 \cdot K) & \text{if } K \ge 1 \\
68.0 & \text{if } K = 0 
\end{cases}$$
Where:
- $K$ is the number of distinct competitor tools named in user complaints (e.g. Jira, Datadog, Salesforce). Mentioning multiple failing incumbents increases the vacuum score.

### Pillar 6: Technical Feasibility Score ($S_{\text{feasibility}}$) — Weight: 5% (Heuristic Estimate)
An architectural heuristic assessing the initial engineering complexity required to build a software wedge:
- SaaS Operations: $90.0$
- Remote Work & Collaboration: $92.0$
- Developer Tools & APIs: $88.0$
- E-Commerce & Workflows: $86.0$
- AI Automation & Agents: $85.0$
- General / Default: $80.0$

*Note: This metric is tagged with a distinct `Heuristic Estimate` badge in the UI.*

### Pillar 7: Evidence Recency Score ($S_{\text{recency}}$) — Weight: 5% (Time Decay)
Temporal decay metric penalizing outdated discussions:
$$S_{\text{recency}} = \begin{cases} 
96.0 & \text{if } \Delta t \le 7 \text{ days} \\
86.0 & \text{if } \Delta t \le 30 \text{ days} \\
74.0 & \text{if } \Delta t \le 90 \text{ days} \\
60.0 & \text{if } \Delta t > 90 \text{ days} \\
85.0 & \text{if publication date unrecorded}
\end{cases}$$

---

## 4. Overall Composite Formula

The Composite Opportunity Score ($S_{\text{overall}}$) is computed as the strictly bounded linear combination:

$$S_{\text{overall}} = 0.25 S_{\text{demand}} + 0.25 S_{\text{pain}} + 0.15 S_{\text{evidence}} + 0.15 S_{\text{wtp}} + 0.10 S_{\text{vacuum}} + 0.05 S_{\text{feasibility}} + 0.05 S_{\text{recency}}$$

$$\sum W_i = 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.15 + 0.15 + 0.10 + 0.05 + 0.05 = 1.00$$

### Score Tier Classifications:
- **85.0 — 100.0**: **High Potential Market Gap** (Deep, acute recurring pain, high commercial intent, proven competitive vacuum).
- **70.0 — 84.9**: **Promising Opportunity** (Moderate to high recurrence, clear product wedge opportunity).
- **50.0 — 69.9**: **Emerging Niche** (Early signal requiring additional evidence corroboration).
- **0.0 — 49.9**: **Low Confidence / Unvalidated** (Insufficient evidence density or weak commercial intent).
